# Limitations

- Each result describes one coarse reanalysis grid cell. Karachi’s coastal cell can blend land and sea. A cell is insufficient for an inference about individual weather stations, neighborhoods, cities as a whole, or the entire province.
- Karachi has the largest full-period fitted slope among these eight cells. Different local conditions, coastal grid composition, and reanalysis characteristics could contribute to the pattern. Comparing the separate confidence intervals gives no direct test of a slope difference. In an exploratory offline check, OLS fits to the matched 1981–2025 annual Karachi-minus-site series used three-lag Newey–West uncertainty and Holm correction across the seven comparisons. The adjusted p-values were below 0.05 for Hyderabad, Badin, Thatta, and Mithi. This post hoc check applies to the selected cells and period. It cannot distinguish physical causes from grid or model effects. The app keeps its interactive comparisons descriptive.
- A linear fit averages change over a window and can miss shifts or extremes. Shorter windows often reduce the information available for estimating a trend.
- Conventional intervals assume independent, constant-variance residuals. Newey–West accounts for short-range dependence and heteroskedasticity. Its approximation depends on the sample and lag rule. In the seeded AR(1) exercise, nominal 95% interval coverage was 265/400 for conventional OLS and 337/400 for HAC. That exercise illustrates one simulated setting.
- Results across eight cells and four related windows are exploratory. The app applies no multiple-comparison adjustment. The intervals quantify fitted trend uncertainty conditional on the model and exclude reanalysis uncertainty. The investigation does not attribute causes.
- The app serves versioned data snapshots. Future NASA revisions can change a refreshed dataset. Giovanni integration and the associated challenge resource requirement remain unresolved in this release.
